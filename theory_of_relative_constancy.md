# The Theory of Relative Constancy

The **Hubble tension**—a persistent, statistically significant discrepancy between early-universe (CMB) and late-universe (supernovae) measurements of cosmic expansion—has resisted every attempt at resolution. Standard responses assume one measurement is wrong or new physics is required. Both presume all measurements recover the *same underlying quantity*.

**But there's a third possibility: physical constants may be constant within a measurement channel but differ systematically between channels.** The Hubble constant H₀ might not be a single number. Instead, there might be H₀^(CMB), H₀^(SNe), and H₀^(GW)—each real, each internally consistent, but indexed to the measurement channel that defines it. This is **channel-dependent measurement**: persistent disagreement doesn't imply error; it can indicate that each method probes the universe through a different physical pathway. This does not deny cosmic expansion, nor does it modify ΛCDM; it questions only the assumption that all high-precision measurements must be directly comparable.

## The Problem

Cosmology does not directly observe distance, expansion, or time. It observes **correlated signals**—angles, redshifts, brightness, timing—through specific physical processes. Distance and expansion are *reconstructed* from these observables.

Different cosmological probes rely on **different physical channels**:

* **CMB:** photon correlations set at recombination
* **Supernovae:** nuclear physics and photon propagation in the late universe
* **Gravitational waves:** spacetime dynamics of compact-object mergers

These channels are not interchangeable. Each constructs an internally coherent cosmic history using different assumptions, correlations, and physical mechanisms.

## Why This Is Not "Systematics vs. New Physics"

Labeling the Hubble tension as "systematic error" or "new physics" presumes that one measurement frame is privileged and correct. That presumption is untested.

Instead, the tension may reflect a **misalignment between measurement channels**, analogous to depth perception: each eye provides a consistent image, but depth emerges from their relationship—not from either image alone. High-precision disagreement between coherent reconstructions may carry information about the structure of measurement itself.

The **Theory of Relative Constancy** proposes that what we're measuring depends on how we're measuring it. Think of measuring a mountain's height by timing rocks rolling down different faces: you'll get different answers depending on slope, friction, and curvature. The mountain has fixed structure, but your measurement is path-dependent. No path gives the "true" height—each gives a real number indexed to the measurement process.

## The Gravitational-Wave Test

Gravitational-wave standard sirens introduce a **third, non-electromagnetic channel**. They do not act as a referee; they act as **triangulation**. A referee presumes a shared frame; triangulation reveals relationships between frames:

* If GW measurements converge with one EM channel and not the other, the discrepancy is likely within the EM sector
* If GW measurements disagree systematically with both EM channels, the discrepancy lies in the **EM–gravity interface**
* If GW measurements fall between them, all channels are internally consistent but mutually misaligned

The framework predicts the **third outcome**: gravitational waves will not resolve the tension, but reveal its structure. This framework is falsified if GW measurements converge precisely with both CMB and supernova results, or if a single cosmological model fits all three simultaneously without channel-specific adjustments.

## Measurement Coherence Does Not Guarantee Commensurability

This framework challenges a deep assumption: **measurement coherence does not guarantee commensurability.** Internally coherent measurements made through different physical channels may be mutually incommensurable—not because one is wrong, but because each probes a different projection of a high-dimensional physical reality.

Different measurement channels construct different temporal orderings and distance scales from fundamentally different correlation structures. When these approximately agree (as in our local universe), we experience them as unified. But at cosmological scales, with extraordinary precision and vastly different physical regimes, there's no guarantee these orderings remain aligned.

The question shifts from *"Which measurement is correct?"* to: **What is the relationship between coherent measurements made through different physical channels?** That relationship may be as fundamental as the quantities we seek to measure.

## Implications Beyond Cosmology (Non-Essential)

This principle generalizes beyond cosmology:

**AI systems** can be internally coherent while misaligned with reality. The model is coherent within its training data channel but may diverge systematically from other channels (direct observation, causal reasoning, physical constraints). The alignment problem becomes: how do you coordinate systems operating with fundamentally different correlation structures?

**Metrological standards** are constant within paradigms, not across them. The speed of light is no longer measured—it's stipulated by definition. If gravitational waves travel at a slightly different speed than electromagnetic waves, we wouldn't detect this by "measuring c"—we'd detect it as an inconsistency between electromagnetic-based and gravitational-based distance scales.

**Quantum mechanics** suggests that measurement outcomes depend on the basis chosen, hinting at a broader principle: what we observe depends on how we probe.

**Cosmological tensions** may signal limits of projection, not failures of theory. The resolution isn't finding which measurement is correct or discovering new physics that reconciles them. It's recognizing that high-precision disagreement between coherent systems isn't a crisis—it's information about the structure of reality and our epistemic relationship to it.

This framework does not claim that constants vary arbitrarily, nor that reality is observer-dependent in a trivial sense. It claims only that high-precision science must account for the fact that measurement is a physical process, not a transparent window.
